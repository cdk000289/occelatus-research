Google changed your hours or business info? How to fix it and stop it.

Customers turn up when you're closed. Your phone number is wrong. Your category changed overnight. If your profile changed and you didn't do it, it was most likely a suggested edit from the public or an automatic Google update. Here is how to spot it, put it back, and make it less likely to happen again.

Updated November 18, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Sign in at business.google.com and open your profile editor. Info shown in blue was changed by Google. When Google tells you about a suggested edit, you have 4 days to accept or reject it, or Google may publish it if other public info (like your website) supports it. Reject wrong edits, correct the field yourself, and make your website and other listings say exactly the same thing.

Why your profile changed

Google says it gathers business info "from different sources, like user reports and licensed content," and that if those sources say your info is wrong or outdated, Google may update your profile. In practice, the usual causes are:

  • A suggested edit. Anyone with a Google account can tap "Suggest an edit" on your listing: a customer, a confused visitor, or occasionally a competitor.
  • Conflicting info elsewhere. Your website, Facebook page or an old directory listing shows different hours or a different phone number, and Google trusts that.
  • A connected tool. A booking, POS or listings app with permission to edit your profile can overwrite hours you set by hand.
  • A policy fix. Google says it may apply edits "without prior review" where they're needed to meet its guidelines, for example a business name with extra keywords in it.

Step 1: Find what changed

Sign in at business.google.com and open the profile editor. Google's help page says text in blue shows "info changed by Google", and you'll see a notification at the top of the page when there are Google updates. Check name, category, hours (including holiday hours), phone, website and address.

Step 2: Accept or reject within 4 days

When a suggested edit needs your review, Google may email you or show a notification. From that point you have 4 days to accept or reject it. If you don't respond, Google "may automatically publish the update if it is supported by other publicly available information, such as your business website." So check your email and the profile at least twice a week.

Step 3: Put the right info back

If a wrong change already went live, edit the field yourself in the profile editor. Google says edits usually take up to 10 minutes to review but can take up to 30 days, and it may not approve a change it can't confirm; in that case you might be able to appeal. Change only what's wrong. Many edits at once can trigger extra review.

Step 4: Make it stick

  • Match everywhere. Make your website, Facebook page and other listings show exactly the same name, address, phone and hours. That's the "other publicly available information" Google checks.
  • Set special hours for holidays and closures in advance, so visitors don't "correct" you.
  • Check connected apps. If a tool syncs to Google, update the hours there too or remove its access.
  • Look weekly. A quick check of the editor and your notifications catches edits inside the 4-day window.
Avoid: making a new profile to "start fresh". That creates a duplicate and can get it suspended (see duplicate listings). If a competitor keeps sabotaging your listing, collect screenshots with dates; see our spam listings guide for how Google takes reports.
