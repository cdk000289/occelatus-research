Google kept the review you reported? How the one-time appeal works.

You reported a fake or unfair review and Google answered "no policy violation". You still have one appeal for that review. Here's which reviews Google actually removes, how to send the appeal, and what to do if the review stays.

Updated November 27, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: If Google says "Report reviewed - no policy violation", you get one appeal per review (up to 10 at a time) in the Reviews Management Tool. Use it only for reviews that clearly break a named Google policy, and say which one and how you know. If the review stays, reply calmly and keep asking every recent customer for a review. Nobody can promise a removal.

What the statuses mean

After you report a review, its status shows in the Reviews Management Tool (Google: report inappropriate reviews):

  • Decision pending: flagged, not looked at yet. Google says evaluation typically takes several days.
  • Report reviewed - no policy violation: Google looked and kept the review. This is where your one-time appeal comes in.
  • Escalated - check your email for updates: your appeal is in. The final decision arrives by email, and the review can't be escalated again.
Appeals are slow right now. Google's Business Profile community has a pinned notice that review appeal processing times are extended because of high volume (Google community: review appeal delays). Don't re-report the same review while you wait.

First, check it really breaks a rule

You only get one appeal per review, so spend it on the ones with a clear policy case. Google removes reviews that break its content policies (Google Maps content policy), for example:

  • Not a real experience: the review doesn't describe a genuine visit or service, or describes something you don't offer.
  • A competitor posting to hurt your reputation.
  • Conflict of interest: Google names current or former employment, a contractual relationship, competitors and family as examples. Reviews from ex-employees fall here.
  • Several accounts, one person: the same person posting from more than one account.
  • Paid or incentivised reviews.
  • Harassment, hate speech, personal information or unsubstantiated allegations of criminal or unethical behaviour.

What Google won't remove: a real customer's honest bad experience, even an unfair-sounding one. Google says it doesn't get involved in disputes between businesses and customers. For those, a calm reply works better than an appeal; our bad review guide has examples.

How to send the appeal (5 minutes)

  1. Open the Reviews Management Tool and confirm the email that manages your profile.
  2. Select your business, then Check the status of a review I reported previously and appeal options.
  3. At the bottom, select Appeal eligible reviews, tick the review (up to 10), then Submit an appeal and fill in the form.
  4. In your own words, name the policy the review breaks and say how you know, in two or three factual sentences. For example: "Conflict of interest: the reviewer is a former employee who left in March; the review was posted the week they left." Keep it calm and specific; don't paste insults or long histories.
  5. Google emails the result. If the review breaks policy it's removed; if not, it stays and shows "Escalated".
Never offer the reviewer anything to take it down. Google doesn't allow discounts, refunds or free services in exchange for removing a negative review. A refund for a genuine complaint is fine, as long as it isn't tied to the review.

If the review stays

  • Reply once, briefly and factually. Future customers read the reply more than the review. If you have no record of the person, say so politely.
  • Keep asking every recent customer for a review, the same way each time. Fresh, genuine reviews push one bad review down. Ask all of them, not only the happy ones; Google doesn't allow picking who to ask.
  • Someone demanding money to remove bad reviews? That's extortion, with its own report form: see our extortion guide.
  • You believe it's defamatory? That's a legal question, not a policy one. Google has a separate legal removal request, and a lawyer can tell you whether it applies.
  • A real customer's good review vanished instead? That's Google's spam filter, a different problem: see reviews disappeared.

Common questions

Can I appeal a review more than once?

No. Google gives each reported review one appeal. You can send up to 10 eligible reviews in one appeal from the Reviews Management Tool. Once a review shows 'Escalated - check your email for updates', the final decision comes by email and it can't be escalated again.

Which reviews will Google remove?

Only reviews that break Google's content policies, for example a review not based on a real experience, one posted by a competitor to hurt you, one from a current or former employee or someone else with a conflict of interest, a paid or incentivised review, harassment, or personal information. Google says it doesn't remove a review just because the business disagrees with it.

How long does a review appeal take?

Google says the first evaluation of a report typically takes several days. In late 2026 Google's Business Profile community also pinned a notice that review appeal processing times are extended because of high volume. Nobody can promise a removal or a date.
