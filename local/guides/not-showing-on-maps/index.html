Business not showing up on Google Maps? Start here.

If customers say they can't find you, the cause is usually one of a few things. Work through this list in order; most people find the problem in the first three checks.

Updated November 8, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: make sure the profile is verified and not suspended, search the way a customer would (not just your name), and check your primary category. Then work on the things that affect ranking: complete information, reviews and recent activity.

1. Check that the profile is verified and live

Sign in to the Google account that manages the profile and look for any notice about verification, suspension or edits under review. An unverified or suspended profile won't show normally, whatever else you do. If it's suspended, see our suspension guide.

2. Search like a customer

Searching your own business name and searching "plumber near me" are different tests. Customers usually search for the service. Open a private browsing window, or ask someone a few miles away, and search for your main service plus your town. Note where you appear and who appears above you.

3. Check your primary category

The primary category has a large effect on which searches you show up for. "Contractor" is much weaker than "Plumber" for someone searching for a plumber. Pick the most specific category that matches your main service, then add secondary categories for other real services.

4. Understand distance

Google weighs how close a business is to the person searching. A business 15 miles away will often appear below closer competitors for "near me" searches, even with a great profile. That's normal. The goal is to be the strongest option in your area, not to show up everywhere.

5. Complete every section

  • Hours, including holiday hours
  • Services or products, with short descriptions
  • A clear business description written for customers
  • Website and booking or contact links
  • Attributes that apply to you

6. Reviews and activity

Profiles with a steady flow of recent reviews, owner replies, new photos and posts tend to look stronger to customers, and Google describes prominence (how well-known and well-reviewed a business is) as one of its local ranking factors. Ask every customer for a review, not just the happy ones, and reply to all of them. See how to reply to negative reviews.

7. Look for duplicates and wrong information

Search for your phone number and address on Google Maps. A duplicate or old listing can split your reviews and confuse Google. Make sure your name, address and phone match exactly across your website and major directories.

Avoid: adding keywords or city names to your business name. It's against Google's guidelines and a common cause of suspensions.
