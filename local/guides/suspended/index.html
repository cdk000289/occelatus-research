Google Business Profile suspended? What to do next.

A suspension can make your listing vanish from Maps overnight. Most are caused by a handful of fixable problems. Here is how to find yours and ask Google to reinstate it properly.

Updated November 8, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: don't create a new listing. Find and fix the likely cause first, gather proof that your business is real and at that address, then submit Google's reinstatement request from your Business Profile. Only the owner or a manager of the profile can submit it.

Suspended or disabled? Check which one first

Google uses different words for different problems, and the fix depends on which one you have. Open your profile while signed in to the Google account that manages it and read the notice carefully.

  • Suspended usually means Google thinks the listing breaks its guidelines. The listing may be hidden from customers or shown with limited information.
  • Disabled is typically about the profile or account itself, for example guideline violations tied to the account or suspicious activity.

Google's own help page, "Fix suspended or disabled profiles", describes the current process and links to the request form. The steps there change from time to time, so treat it as the source of truth.

The most common causes

  1. Extra words in the business name. "Smith Plumbing – Best Emergency Plumber Austin" breaks the rules. Use your real-world name, exactly as it appears on your sign, invoices and website.
  2. An address Google doesn't accept. Virtual offices, mailboxes, PO boxes and coworking desks you don't staff during business hours often trigger suspensions.
  3. Service-area business showing a home address. If customers don't come to you, the address should be hidden and a service area set instead.
  4. Duplicate listings. Two profiles for the same business at the same place, often created by a past employee or agency.
  5. Big edits at once. Changing name, address and category together can send a profile into review.
  6. Wrong category. A category that doesn't match what you actually do.

Fix the cause before you appeal

Requests that leave the original problem in place tend to fail. Before you submit:

  • Rename the profile to your exact real-world business name.
  • Remove or hide any address that isn't a staffed location customers can visit.
  • Make sure the phone number and website belong to this business and match each other.
  • Don't make new edits while the request is being reviewed.

Documents to have ready

Google may ask for proof. Collect these before you start so you can upload them straight away:

  • Business license or registration showing the business name.
  • A recent utility bill or lease with the business name and address.
  • Photos of your storefront with permanent signage, or for service businesses, photos of branded vehicles or equipment.
Avoid: creating a new profile to "start over" (it creates a duplicate and usually makes things worse), paying anyone who "guarantees" reinstatement, and submitting several requests in a row. Nobody outside Google can guarantee the outcome.

How long it takes

It varies. Some requests are answered within days, others take weeks, and Google may ask for more information by email. Keep an eye on the inbox of the account that manages the profile.
