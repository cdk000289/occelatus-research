Salon suite or booth renter? Your own Google listing, without a duplicate flag.

You rent a suite or a chair, you have your own clients, your own booking page and your own reviews. But Google sees one building with a lot of businesses in it. Here's when Google lets you have your own listing at a shared address, how to set it up so it isn't treated as a duplicate, and what to do if it already was.

Updated November 26, 2026 · For stylists, barbers, estheticians, nail and lash artists, massage therapists

Short version: Use the name on your door, the building address with your suite number on line 2, your own phone number, and hours you're actually there. Have permanent signage with your business name. If Google marks you as a duplicate, appeal with photos of your signage; never make a second profile. Google decides on every profile, so nobody can promise yours stays up.

Can you have your own listing at a shared address?

Google's rules allow it in two ways:

  • Separate businesses at one address can each have a profile if they are "distinct businesses with different names and clearly visible differences in signage" (Google: resolve duplicate profiles).
  • Individual practitioners at a shared location can have their own profile if they are public-facing and "can be contacted directly at the verified location during stated hours" (Google: guidelines for representing your business). Google's examples are doctors, lawyers and agents; it doesn't name stylists, so how this applies to you is Google's call.

Suite renter (your own lockable room, your own door sign): you usually fit the first case. Chair or booth renter inside someone else's salon, with no sign of your own: a second profile at the salon's address is much more likely to be flagged, because the salon's profile already represents that spot. Check your rental agreement too; some restrict separate listings.

Don't fake it. Google doesn't allow virtual offices, mailboxes, or a co-working desk without signage and staff (same Google page). Only list an address where clients actually come to you.

Set it up so it reads as its own business

  1. Name: exactly what's on your door, business cards and booking page. No keywords ("Best Balayage Austin"), and no "inside" or "at" the building: Google lists containment like "Geek Squad (inside Best Buy)" as not allowed in a name.
  2. Address: the building's street address on line 1, your suite number on line 2. Google asks for exactly this layout.
  3. Phone: your own number that reaches you, not the building's front desk. Google says the number must be under the business's direct control.
  4. Hours: the hours you're actually in the suite and reachable, not the building's opening hours.
  5. Signage: Google says businesses that show their address should keep permanent, fixed signage with their name there. A printed sheet on the door is weak evidence; a proper door plaque or sign with your name and suite number is better.
  6. Category: the fewest categories that describe your core work (Google's own advice), for example Hair salon, Nail salon, Eyelash salon.
  7. Book button: point it at your own booking page, not the building's. Our booking button guide covers how.

Already marked as a duplicate, or merged with someone else?

  1. Don't create a new profile. Google allows one profile per business; a second one is itself a duplicate.
  2. Collect evidence: clear photos of your permanent sign with your name and suite number, the building directory if there is one, and the sign of the other business you were merged with. Google says that when both businesses serve customers at the same location, it asks for evidence of permanent signage clearly showing both. Your suite lease page or business license can help show you're a separate business.
  3. Appeal the duplicate status through Business Profile support. Both businesses must be eligible and distinct.
  4. If two profiles of yours were merged on purpose (same business, two listings), note Google's warning: reviews are combined but replies to reviews may be lost.

Moving to another suite or building

Edit the address on your existing profile; don't start a new one. Google says the same in its duplicates help (Google: resolve duplicate profiles). Your reviews stay with the profile. Change your sign, website and booking page on the same day so they all match. More in our moving guide.

Common questions

Can I have my own Google listing in a salon suite building?

Often yes. Google allows several businesses at one address to each have a profile if they are distinct businesses with different names and clearly visible differences in signage, and it allows individual practitioners at a shared location who are public-facing and can be reached at that location during their stated hours. Google makes the final call on each profile.

Why was my listing marked as a duplicate?

One of the reasons Google gives for marking a profile as a duplicate is that multiple profiles have the same address. Suite buildings and salons with chair renters put many profiles on one address, so names, suite numbers, phone numbers and signage need to make each business clearly distinct.

How do I appeal a duplicate or merged listing?

Contact Google Business Profile support to appeal the duplicate status. Both businesses must be eligible and distinct, and if both serve customers at the same location Google asks for evidence of permanent signage that clearly shows both businesses. Don't create a new profile to get around it.
