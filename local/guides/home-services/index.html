Guide · Home-service trades

Locksmith, garage door, HVAC or plumbing? Why Google checks your profile harder.

Some trades have been flooded with fake listings run by call centres and lead sellers. Google responds by looking harder at everyone in those categories, honest local businesses included. Here is what that means and how to set your profile up so it passes.

Updated November 14, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: In high-scrutiny trades, expect video verification, slower edits and less room for mistakes. Use your real business name, list as a service-area business if customers don't come to you, keep proof of the business on hand, and remember that Google's ad verification is a separate process from your profile.

1. Which trades get the extra checks

Local search write-ups consistently name locksmiths, garage door repair, HVAC, plumbing, water damage restoration, towing and movers among the categories with the most spam history, and so the most scrutiny (RankLocalToday, Plyrium). Google doesn't publish a list for Business Profiles, so treat this as a pattern, not a rule. Google does publish one for ads: all US and Canadian locksmiths and US garage door services must pass Advanced Verification to appear in Local Services ads (Google Ads Help).

2. Be ready for video verification

Video is the method Google recommends when you're eligible (Google). Most trades are service-area businesses, and for those Google's video verification page asks for one unedited, live recording of at least 30 seconds, made from a phone through your profile, that shows:

  • where you operate: street signs or landmarks at your business address (from a home base, your immediate neighbourhood is fine);
  • that the business exists: your tools, equipment, workspace, business cards or branded clothing;
  • that you run it: you doing the work or unlocking a branded van, or a permit, invoice or utility bill in the business name.

Leave out bank, tax and ID numbers and other people's faces. Plan the shot before you press record. Our verification failed guide covers why videos get rejected.

3. Set the profile up the way Google expects

  • Use your real business name. Adding cities or services to the name ("Best 24/7 Locksmith Dallas") is against Google's guidelines and a common suspension trigger in these trades.
  • Hide your address if customers don't come to you. Google says a service-area business, such as a locksmith or plumber, should not show its address (Google). See our service-area guide.
  • One profile per real business. Extra listings in nearby cities are exactly what Google is hunting for. See duplicate listings.
  • Change one thing at a time. In these categories, edits can be held for review. Changing name, category and address together looks like a different business.

4. Ads verification is a separate process

A verified Business Profile doesn't clear you for Google's ads. Google says its Advanced Verification for advertisers is different from Business Profile verification and typically takes about 6–8 weeks (Google Ads Help). If you plan to run Local Services ads, start it early.

Avoid: anyone selling "verified" listings, extra profiles in other cities, or a guaranteed ranking. Only Google verifies and ranks profiles, and fake listings are what got these trades extra scrutiny in the first place.
