Does ChatGPT recommend your business? What owners can actually control.

More customers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI "who's a good plumber near me?" instead of scrolling a list. Plenty of owners have tried it and found their competitor named and themselves missing. Here is what these answers are built from, what nobody can honestly promise you, and the free steps that give you the best chance.

Updated November 20, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: No one controls what an AI recommends, and anyone who guarantees you a spot is overpromising. What you can control is the information these tools read: a complete, accurate Google Business Profile, the same name, address, phone and hours on Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and Facebook, recent reviews with replies, and a website that says plainly what you do and where. It's the same work that helps you show up on Google Maps.

Why it matters now

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of US consumers found that use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local business recommendations jumped from 6% to 45% in a year, making AI the third most used source after Google and Facebook. Google is still far ahead, but its share fell from 83% to 71%, and Apple Maps nearly doubled to 27% (BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026). People don't use just one place anymore: the same survey found the average consumer uses six review sites.

Owners are noticing. Threads like "Tested if ChatGPT recommends my business (it doesn't)" and "ChatGPT recommended my competitor by name" keep coming up. Others find AI quoting prices or hours they changed long ago.

What these answers are built from

  • Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode are part of Google Search. Google says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations needed to appear, and its advice to site owners includes keeping your Business Profile information up to date. Local AI answers often show ratings, review counts and hours from Business Profiles.
  • ChatGPT searches the web and, for "near me" questions, uses outside providers of business listings that OpenAI doesn't fully name. Owners of existing US listings can now claim their business in ChatGPT's Business Hub, but OpenAI says plainly that claiming doesn't guarantee more visibility and that advertising doesn't influence its answers.
  • Different AI tools read different sources. You can't know which listing a given tool will read, so the safe move is to make all of them say the same thing.
Be careful who you pay: "Get recommended by ChatGPT" services are springing up fast. Nobody can guarantee an AI will name your business. And the shortcuts some sell, such as fake reviews or keywords stuffed into your business name, can get your Google profile suspended (see profile suspended). If a pitch promises a guaranteed spot, walk away.

Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile complete and correct

It is still the biggest single source of local facts. Check the basics: the right primary category, real hours (including holiday hours), phone, website, service area, a description that says what you do and where, and current photos. If Google has changed something without asking you, see when Google changes your info.

Step 2: Make every other listing say the same thing

Search your business name on Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp and Facebook. Each keeps its own copy of your details. Claim the ones you can (Apple Business Connect, Bing Places and Yelp for Business all have free listings) and fix any old phone number, address or hours. If you've moved, this matters twice as much: see moved to a new address.

Step 3: Keep reviews recent, and reply to them

AI answers often sum up what reviewers say. A steady trickle of honest reviews from real customers, each with a short reply, gives them something current and specific to work with. Never buy reviews or offer anything in return for them: that breaks Google's rules, and fake reviews are illegal under the FTC's rule. Our bad review guide has reply examples you can adapt.

Step 4: Make your website say it plainly

On your home page, say in normal words what you do, the towns you serve, your hours and your phone number, matching your listings. If ChatGPT quotes an old price or service, check whether that old information still sits on your site or on an old listing somewhere.

Step 5: Claim your ChatGPT listing if you want to

It's free. In ChatGPT, open Business Hub from the sidebar, search your business name, and verify by business phone, business email or a website DNS record. To publish changes you also need an ID check through OpenAI's partner Persona. Local stores and restaurants can currently only claim a listing that already exists in the US. Only the owner or someone they authorize can do this, and claiming alone won't move you up.

How to check

Ask ChatGPT and Google (in AI Mode) the question a customer would, such as "best [your trade] in [your town]", in a private browser window. Ask again in a month. Answers vary from person to person and day to day, so look for a trend, not one answer.
