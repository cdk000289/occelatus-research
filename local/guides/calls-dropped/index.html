Guide · Fewer calls

Fewer calls from Google this year? How to tell a layout change from a real problem.

The phone is quieter and the calls line in your Google Business Profile is sloping down. Before you blame a competitor or pay someone to "fix your ranking", check what actually changed. Some of it is Google moving its buttons and counting differently. Some of it can be a real problem with your listing that you can fix.

Updated November 23, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Google keeps changing how local results look on phones, and some layouts make it harder to call you in one tap. Compare this year's months with each other, not with old reports. If searches and direction requests held up while calls fell, the way people reach you changed, not your listing. If searches fell too, check the listing itself: verified, right category, right phone number, correct hours, recent reviews. Those you can fix.

What changed on Google's side

  • Fewer one-tap call buttons in some results. Several marketing agencies report that on phones, some local results no longer show a Call button on each business, so a customer has to open your profile first (for example this write-up). Google hasn't announced this, and it varies by search, so check it yourself (Step 2 below).
  • A test with no buttons at all. In November 2026, Search Engine Roundtable reported and screenshotted a Google test where an AI-written local result on phones showed business names with no call, website or directions buttons, and no reviews or photos. By November 18 the normal version was showing again for some people. It was a test, but it shows the direction.
  • Google stopped keeping a call log for you. Since July 31, 2024, the call history feature is no longer in your Business Profile. You still get the calls, and the Performance report still counts taps on your phone number, but you can't see who called.
  • More answers before anyone taps. Google shows ads and AI answers above many local results, and more customers ask ChatGPT and similar tools: BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier. See our AI answers guide.
  • Buttons you didn't add. In November 2026 some owners found a "Book online" link on their profile that they never set up (reported here). If it points to a contact page, a customer who would have called may fill in a form instead, so check your inbox too.

Step 1: Read your own numbers the right way (5 minutes)

  1. Open your profile's Performance report. Look at searches, calls, website clicks and direction requests separately.
  2. Compare recent months with earlier months of this year, and the same season where you can. A quiet November after a busy summer isn't a problem by itself.
  3. Read the pattern:
    • Searches steady, directions or website clicks steady or up, calls down: people still find you; the way they contact you changed. Go to Step 3.
    • Searches down too: fewer people are seeing you. Go to Step 2 and Step 4.
    • Everything dropped to near zero in one week: check whether the profile was suspended, marked closed or changed by someone else. See suspended, marked closed and Google changed my info.

Step 2: Look at Google the way a customer does (10 minutes)

  1. On your phone, in a private/incognito window, search for what customers search: "plumber near me", "[your trade] [your town]".
  2. Do it once near your shop and once from a part of your service area that's farther away. Results change with distance, so the view from your desk is the least useful one.
  3. Note what sits above you: ads, an AI answer, other businesses. And whether your result shows a Call button or only your name.
  4. Open your own profile and tap every button: Call, Website, Directions, and Book if it's there. Does each one go where it should?

Step 3: Make the second tap easy

  • Your phone number: the right number, answered in business hours, with voicemail that says who you are. The same number on your profile and your website.
  • Your website: phone number at the top of the page, tappable on a phone. Many "lost" calls now go through your site.
  • Your hours: correct, including holiday hours. A customer who sees "Closed" won't call.
  • Booking link: if a "Book online" link appeared that you didn't want, check where it goes and change or remove it in your profile's booking settings.

Step 4: If fewer people see you, check the listing

  • Is the profile verified, with no warning in your dashboard?
  • Is the primary category the main thing you do? A wrong category quietly limits which searches you show up for.
  • Do name, address and phone match your website exactly?
  • Are new reviews still coming in, and are you replying? A stalled review count next to a competitor getting reviews every week is a common reason to slip. See our reviews guide.
  • Recent photos and posts: not a ranking guarantee, but they show customers you're open and active.
Be careful who you pay. Nobody can guarantee a spot in Google's results or bring back a Call button Google removed. Be wary of anyone who calls you saying your listing "will be removed" or offering a "Google guaranteed" ranking; Google says its own callers don't ask for payment or promise placement.
