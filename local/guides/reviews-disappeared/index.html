Guide · Missing reviews

Google reviews disappeared? Why it happens and how to ask for them back.

You had 48 reviews last week and now you have 44. Or a happy customer swears they left a review and it never showed up. Google doesn't tell you which review went or why. Here is how to work out what happened, when it's worth asking for a review back, and how to lose fewer next time.

Updated November 22, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: First check whether the review is really gone or just not displaying. Google filters reviews every day, and its filter removes some genuine ones. Google says reviews removed for breaking its rules won't be restored, but if a real customer's honest review was removed by mistake you can ask Google support to look at it. To lose fewer: reply to reviews, ask customers one at a time (not in a big batch), and never pay, discount or ask only happy customers.

Step 1: Gone, delayed or just not showing?

  • A customer says they posted and nothing appears. Google checks every review before publishing it, and says this can take a few days. Customers on old phones or an old Maps app can also have trouble posting. Wait a few days before you worry.
  • Your review count is higher than the reviews you can scroll through, or the customer can still see the review on their own Google profile: that is usually a display problem, not a removal. Google has had several of these in the last two years and most reviews came back. Keep a screenshot and wait.
  • The count dropped and the review is gone from the customer's own profile too: it was removed. It won't come back by itself.
  • You merged two profiles or were just reinstated after a suspension: Google says merged reviews can take a few days to show, and if reviews went missing after a reinstatement you should contact support.
  • Customers see "Posting reviews is turned off for this place" or a warning banner: Google has restricted reviews on the whole profile. Jump to If reviews are turned off.

Why Google removes real reviews

Google runs an automated spam filter on every review. For 2025 it says it blocked or removed over 292 million reviews. That filter catches fake reviews, but Google itself says it may mistakenly remove legitimate ones.

A 2026 study by the review software company Reviewflowz, which tracked about 9,000 Google profiles every day for two weeks, found (their write-up):

  • about 2% of new reviews were removed in their first week, most of them around days 2 to 7;
  • one-star reviews were removed more often than five-star ones, so on average the rating barely moved;
  • a reviewer's very first Google review was far more likely to go than one from an experienced reviewer;
  • a burst of reviews on a single day raised the risk; a busy month did not;
  • five-star reviews that already had an owner reply by day 2 were removed less often.

That is one company's data, and it sells review tools, so treat the exact numbers as a guide. But it matches what owners report: losing a few reviews is normal, and it hits profiles that are actively collecting them.

The things that get reviews removed for good: offering a discount, freebie or payment for a review; asking only happy customers ("review gating"); pushing customers to review while they're still in the shop; staff quotas; reviews from family, staff or yourself. Google's review rules ban these, and paying for fake reviews is also illegal under the FTC's rule. Reviews removed for these reasons won't come back, and repeat problems can get reviews turned off on your whole profile.

Step 2: Decide if it's worth asking for

Ask only when you're sure the review was real: a customer you can name, a job or visit you can date, and nothing in the text that breaks Google's rules. Don't ask for a review you got with an incentive or by only asking happy customers. One more thing: agencies report that when support restores reviews, it may restore other removed reviews on the profile that meet the rules too, including old negative ones.

Step 3: Ask Google support to look at it

  1. Collect the details: the reviewer's name as it showed on Google, the star rating, roughly when it was posted and when it disappeared, and the text if you have it.
  2. The best proof: ask the customer to open their own Google Maps profile, go to their Reviews tab and send you a screenshot.
  3. Find your Business Profile ID (in your profile's settings, under Advanced settings).
  4. Go to Google Business Profile support, signed in as an owner or manager, pick the business, and describe the issue as a missing review. Go past the suggested articles to the contact options.
  5. Google's first answer is often an automatic email listing the usual causes. That isn't a no. Reply to it with your details and proof, and keep the case number.

Google decides. Some cases succeed, many don't. If Google says the review broke a rule, it stays down.

If reviews are turned off on your profile

If Google decides a profile was getting fake reviews, it says it emails the owner and can stop new reviews for a time, hide existing reviews for a time, or show customers a warning. Google has also announced it can pause new reviews after a sudden spike of spam, as protection. Read Google's email carefully, stop whatever caused it (a review contest, a batch request, a vendor "boosting" reviews), and use the appeal option Google gives you. Don't open a second profile to get around it: that's a fast way to a suspension.

Step 4: Lose fewer reviews from now on

  • Ask one customer at a time, right after the job or sale, instead of emailing your whole list on one day.
  • Reply to reviews, good and bad, within a day or two. Our bad review guide has reply examples.
  • Ask everyone, not just the happy ones. A mix of 4s and 5s looks real; a wall of perfect 5s is what the filter looks at harder.
  • Keep your own copy. Once a month, screenshot or note your reviews. Google won't tell you which one went, so your copy is your only record.
