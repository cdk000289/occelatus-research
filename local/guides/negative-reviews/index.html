How to respond to a negative Google review, with examples.

A bad review stings, but your reply is written for the next customer reading it, not just the reviewer. A good reply can turn a 1-star review into a reason to call you.

Updated November 8, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: reply within a day or two, stay calm, thank them, acknowledge the issue without arguing, take the details offline, and never share private customer information. You can report reviews that break Google's rules, but you can't remove honest negative ones.

Why replying matters

In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 89% of consumers said they expect business owners to respond to reviews, and 80% said they're likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews. Replying only to the bad ones, or only to the good ones, did much worse.

A simple structure

  1. Thank them for taking the time to write.
  2. Acknowledge what went wrong, in their words, without making excuses.
  3. Say what you're doing about it, if anything has changed.
  4. Take it offline with a direct phone number or email.
  5. Keep it short. Three to five sentences is plenty.

Example replies you can adapt

These are examples, not replies from real businesses.

Late arrivalHi Dana, thank you for letting us know, and I'm sorry we were two hours late without calling ahead. That's not how we want to treat anyone. We've changed how our team updates customers when a job runs over. I'd like to make this right, so please call me directly at (555) 010-0100. – Mike, owner
Price complaintHi Chris, thanks for the honest feedback. I'm sorry the final bill felt higher than you expected. We should have walked you through the extra parts before starting. I'd be glad to go over the invoice with you line by line; you can reach me at office@example.com. – Lisa
Review you can't match to a customerHi Sam, thank you for taking the time to write. We take every review seriously, but we can't find a visit matching these details. Could you call us at (555) 010-0100 so we can look into it properly? – The team

What never to do

  • Argue, blame the customer or reply in anger. Wait an hour if you need to.
  • Share private details about the customer or the job. For health, dental and similar businesses this can also create legal problems, so don't confirm someone was a patient.
  • Offer money, discounts or gifts to change or remove a review.
  • Post fake positive reviews to balance it out. It breaks Google's rules, and the FTC's rule on consumer reviews (16 CFR Part 465) bans fake reviews and allows significant fines.

Can you get a review removed?

Only if it breaks Google's policies, for example spam, off-topic content, conflicts of interest (like a competitor or ex-employee), or offensive language. You can report it from your Business Profile; Google decides. Honest negative reviews stay up, which is why a good reply matters so much.

The long-term fix: more reviews

One bad review hurts most when you only have a few. Ask every customer for a review after each job, with your direct review link. Ask everyone, not just people you expect to be happy: only asking happy customers ("review gating") is against Google's policies.
