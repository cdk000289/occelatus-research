Google Business Profile verification failed? What to try next.

Verification is the step where many new profiles get stuck, and video verification in particular. Here is what Google is looking for, why attempts get rejected, and what to do after one does.

Updated November 9, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Google wants proof that the business really operates where the profile says it does. Most rejected videos fail because they don't show a continuous path from the street to the business, don't show real signage or equipment, or the name and address on the profile don't match what the video shows. Fix the mismatch first, then record again.

What video verification asks for

Google's own help page, "Verify your business profile", is the source of truth and its requirements change from time to time. In general the video should show, in one continuous take:

  • The location and its surroundings, such as the street, nearby signs or landmarks.
  • Your business name on signage, or for a service business, branded vehicles or equipment.
  • Proof you can access the place, such as unlocking the door or entering the premises.
  • Evidence of the business operating there, such as tools, stock, a workspace or business documents.

Why videos get rejected

Owners in public help forums describe the same few causes again and again:

  1. Name mismatch. The name on the profile, the sign and the paperwork aren't the same.
  2. No visible branding. A plain unit or vehicle with nothing that shows the business name.
  3. The video is cut or edited. It needs to be one continuous recording.
  4. Service-area business with the wrong setup. If customers don't visit you, the address should be hidden and a service area set. A mismatch between the address and the service area is a commonly reported error.
  5. A shared or virtual address. Mailboxes, virtual offices and coworking desks are hard or impossible to verify.

Before you record again

  • Make the profile name match the real-world name on your sign, invoices and website exactly.
  • Confirm the address is a place you can physically access, and that the service-area setting matches how you work.
  • Put up temporary branded signage or use a branded vehicle if you have none visible. Only show what is genuinely yours.
  • Record it in one take, in good light, in the order Google lists.
Avoid: filming a location that isn't really yours, borrowing someone else's signage, creating a new profile to try again (it creates a duplicate), and paying anyone who guarantees verification. Nobody outside Google can guarantee the outcome.

If it is rejected again

Use the "Contact us" or review option inside the Business Profile to ask for another review or another verification method, and read the message Google gives you carefully. You can also ask in Google's Business Profile Help Community, where volunteer Product Experts can escalate cases. Results vary and Google makes the final call.
