Bad or wrong photos on your Google listing? How to remove or replace them.

The first photo people see is a blurry customer shot, a storefront from before you renovated, a dish you stopped serving, or a picture that isn't your business at all. Or you set a cover photo and Google shows something else. Here's what you can delete, what you can only report, and how to get the right photos in front.

Updated November 25, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Photos you uploaded you can delete any time. Photos customers uploaded you can't delete; you can only report them, and Google removes them only if they break its content rules (review can take several business days). The best fix for an unflattering but allowed photo is to add better ones of your own, with a sharp cover photo and logo.

Two kinds of photos on your profile

  • Yours. Photos and videos you (or a manager) added. You can delete them from the Photos tab: open the photo, select Delete, confirm (Google's help page).
  • Customers'. Photos posted with reviews or as "photo updates" in Google Maps. You can't stop customers posting them and you can't delete them (Google: photo updates from customers). You can report one if it breaks Google's rules.

Reporting a customer photo

  1. Open your Business Profile, select Photos › View all photos, and open the photo.
  2. Select Report a problem (on the Maps app: More › Report).
  3. Pick the reason that really fits, for example "Not a photo or video of the place", "Privacy concern", "Spam" or "Personal information".
  4. Submit once. Google says sending several reports for the same photo can delay the review (Google: report inappropriate photos).

Photos that usually qualify: a different business or place, spam or ads, offensive content, and photos showing someone's personal information. A photo that's simply unflattering, old or low quality usually stays if it's a real photo of your business; see Google's photo and video policy.

Nobody can promise a removal. Google decides on every report. Be wary of anyone who charges to "guarantee" a photo comes down.

The 10-minute photo check

  1. On your phone, search your business name in Google and in Google Maps. Look at the first 3 photos people see: do they show your business as it is today?
  2. Check your logo and cover photo. Google says choosing a cover doesn't guarantee it shows first, and a low-quality cover may be swapped for a customer photo.
  3. Delete your own photos that are out of date: old signage, a previous address, products or services you no longer offer, staff who left.
  4. Add a few new ones: the outside (so people recognise you on arrival), the inside, your work or products, and your team if they agree. Google recommends JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, ideally 720 × 720 px or larger, in focus and without heavy filters. New photos can take 24–48 hours to appear.
  5. Report customer photos that clearly break the rules, once each, and note the date.

Special cases

  • A competitor or stranger keeps posting bad photos. Report each one, then see our competitor spam guide for how to document a pattern.
  • A photo shows a client or a staff member who didn't agree. Report it with the privacy or personal-information reason. If it's your own upload, delete it.
  • Salons, clinics, studios: only post client photos with their OK, and keep a note of who agreed.
  • A customer posted a fair photo with a complaint. Reply to it in the Google Maps app the way you would to a review; our review guide has examples.

Common questions

Can I delete a photo a customer posted on my Google listing?

No. You can delete photos you uploaded yourself, but customer photos can only be reported. Google reviews the report, which can take several business days, and removes the photo only if it breaks Google's content policies. Reporting the same photo again can slow the review down.

Why does Google show a different cover photo from the one I chose?

Google says setting a cover photo doesn't guarantee it shows first. If your cover photo is low quality, or other signals suggest a different photo represents the business better, Google may show a customer photo instead. A sharp, well-lit, unfiltered photo of the outside or the work you do gives yours the best chance.

Will new photos get me more customers or a higher ranking?

We can't promise that. Up-to-date photos help people recognise your business and see what you offer, but nobody controls where Google ranks you.
