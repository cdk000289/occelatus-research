Google Business Profile posts rejected? Why it happens and how to fix it.

A post that says "Not approved" won't show to customers on Search or Maps. Most rejections come from a handful of fixable issues. Here is how to find the cause and put it right.

Updated November 15, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Find the rejection reason Google shows you, fix that one thing, and resubmit. Don't keep changing random parts of the post. The most common causes are phone numbers in the post body, unverified contact info, AI-generated or stock photos, restricted content, and links that don't match the post.

First: is it really rejected?

Google shows different statuses for posts. Open your Business Profile and check the post before changing anything:

  • Pending means it's still processing or the profile may not be verified. Wait, and make sure verification is complete.
  • Not approved means Google found a content-policy issue. Look for the question-mark icon next to the status — it often says which policy was triggered.
  • Archived means the post is older than six months. It's not rejected; it's just past its display window unless a date range is set.

Google's help page "Create and manage Posts on your Business Profile" describes the current statuses and options.

The most common causes of rejected posts

  1. Phone numbers in the post body. Google warns that a phone number in the description may cause rejection if it can't be verified as connected to the business. Use the built-in "Call now" button instead of writing the number into the text.
  2. Unverified contact info. Email addresses and social-media handles in the post can also trigger rejection for the same reason. Keep contact details on the profile itself, not in every post.
  3. AI-generated or heavily edited images. Google's 2026 photo policy requires images that represent the real business. Filters that detect generated or heavily altered images can reject the post. Use real photos of your work, storefront or team.
  4. Links that don't match. The destination URL should load, be safe, and match what the post promises. Sending an offer post to a generic homepage when the post is about a specific service can cause problems.
  5. Restricted content. Posts about alcohol, tobacco, pharmaceuticals, gambling, firearms, adult services or financial products face extra rules. If your business is in one of these categories, check Google's content policies before posting.
  6. Misleading claims or fake urgency. "Best in town", "guaranteed results" or "this week only" with no real deadline can be flagged. State what you actually do and back up any claim.
  7. Poor-quality or low-value text. Excessive capitalisation, distracting characters ("!!!"), or posts with very little useful information can be rejected. Write clearly and say something a customer would want to know.

How to fix a rejected post

Save the original post and change one thing at a time. If you change the text, image, link and dates all at once, the next approval or rejection tells you nothing.

  • Start with plain, factual copy and no contact info in the body.
  • If that's approved, add your real photo.
  • Then add the correct link and supported action button.
  • Then set the post type, dates and offer terms if applicable.

Write down what you changed and what happened at each step.

An example

An HVAC contractor posts an offer: "ROOF REPAIRS AVAILABLE NOW!!! CALL 07123 456789!!! Email roofer@example.com Click here for details." The post is rejected. The causes: a phone number in the body, excessive punctuation, an unverified email address, and a vague link destination.

The fix: rewrite as "We have additional roof-repair availability across the area this month. Recent work has included slipped tiles, storm damage and leaking flat roofs. See our roof-repair service page for information and how to request an assessment." Add a "Learn more" button pointing to the roof-repair page. Use a real photo of completed work. No phone number, no email, no exclamation marks.

Will rejected posts suspend my profile?

A rejected post does not automatically mean your profile will be suspended. Google has not published a retry count that triggers suspension. But repeated policy-violating content or blind resubmission of the same post can lead to feature-access restrictions. Fix the cause rather than hitting "Publish" over and over.

Avoid: repeatedly resubmitting the same post without changes, using tracking phone numbers Google can't connect to your business, and AI-generated images that don't show your real business. None of these help your profile.
