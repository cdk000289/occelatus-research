Guide · Competitor spam

Fake or keyword-stuffed competitor on Google Maps? How to report it.

Listings with made-up addresses, names like "Best Plumber Austin 24/7 Emergency", or five "locations" that are really one van are common in trades. Google gives you two ways to report them. Here is which to use, what evidence helps, and what to expect.

Updated November 16, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: For a single wrong detail (a keyword-stuffed name, a fake address), use Suggest an edit on the listing in Google Maps. For listings you believe are fraudulent or don't exist, or for several at once, use Google's Business Redressal Complaint Form with screenshots. Report only real guideline breaks, keep your case number, and work on your own profile at the same time: Google can be slow, and reports don't always lead to removal.

First: does it actually break Google's rules?

Only report what Google's own guidelines forbid. Reporting a competitor just because they rank above you wastes your time and won't work. Google's guidelines for representing your business say a business name must be the real-world name used on signs, website and paperwork, and that "including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted." Common real violations:

  • Keyword-stuffed names. "Smith Plumbing – Emergency Plumber Austin" when the sign and website just say "Smith Plumbing".
  • Fake addresses. A pin at a virtual office, a mailbox store or an empty lot where no one works and no customers are served.
  • Fake extra locations. Several listings across a city for one business that really has one base.
  • Listings for businesses that don't exist, often with stock photos and a call-tracking number.

A competitor having more reviews, a better-looking profile or a nearby address is not a violation.

Option 1: Suggest an edit (one wrong detail)

For a single fixable detail, such as a stuffed name or a wrong address, open the listing in Google Maps, choose Suggest an edit, and correct it (for a name, enter the real name from their sign or website). Google's help page "Edit business information in Google Maps" covers the steps. Owners on Reddit report that edits on names sometimes stick within days and sometimes are ignored.

Option 2: Report misleading or fraudulent listings

If you believe a listing may not exist at all, Google's "Report a business on Google Maps" page says to open it in Maps, choose Suggest an edit → Place is closed or not here → Offensive, harmful, or misleading, and submit.

For misleading names, phone numbers or website links that suggest fraud, the same page points to the Business Redressal Complaint Form. Google says you can report several businesses at once there, including by uploading a spreadsheet of their URLs.

What evidence helps

  • The Google Maps link for each listing.
  • Screenshots of the listing name next to the name on their website, sign or state business registration.
  • A Street View screenshot of the address showing no signage or no such business.
  • For a network of fake listings: the shared phone numbers, websites or photos that tie them together.

Keep it factual and short. Write down the date you submitted and any case number Google sends back.

What to expect

Google decides, and it can be slow. Owners in r/GoogleMyBusiness describe both outcomes: some spam listings removed after a well-documented redressal report, and others reported for months with no action, or removed and then back again. Nobody can promise removal, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

Avoid: reporting competitors who aren't breaking the rules, mass-reporting the same listing from many accounts, leaving fake reviews on their listing, or copying their tactics. The last one is how your own profile gets suspended.

The part you control: your own profile

While a report is pending, make sure your own profile is complete and accurate: the right primary category, a correct address or service area, real photos, current hours and replies to your reviews.
