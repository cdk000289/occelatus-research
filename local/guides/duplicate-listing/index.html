Duplicate Google Business Profile listing? How to find it, merge it or get it removed.

Two profiles for one business split your reviews and confuse customers, and an extra profile you created can look like a rule break to Google. Here is the plain-English route through it.

Updated November 11, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: Find every profile for your business, work out whether you own each one, then either claim it, remove it, or ask Google Business Profile support to merge or remove it. Don't make a third profile to "start fresh".

1. Find the duplicates

Search your business name, phone number and address on Google Maps. Note every result that points to the same business, including old addresses and misspelled names. Write down which Google account (if any) manages each one.

2. Work out which one is yours

  • You can sign in to it: you own it. Keep the one with the most history and accurate details.
  • You can't sign in, but it's your business: on Maps, open it, choose "Claim this business" or "Manage now", and pick a verification method. This is the route Google describes in its help pages.
  • It belongs to someone else (a former agency or employee): you can request ownership, and the current owner gets a notice. If they don't answer, Google's process takes over.

3. Remove or merge it

Google's help page "Resolve duplicate profiles & ownership issues" explains the options. In short: once you control both, you can ask Business Profile support to merge them or to remove the extra one. If the extra is for an old location, marking it permanently closed is another option. If two distinct, eligible businesses were merged by mistake, the same page says to contact support to appeal. Google decides, and timing varies.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Creating another profile because the old one is stuck. It makes the problem bigger.
  • Reporting a competitor's profile without checking. Real separate businesses at one address are not always duplicates.
  • Changing the name or address on the wrong profile and triggering a re-verification.
Avoid: anyone who guarantees a merge or removal. Only Google can do it, and no one outside Google can promise the result.
