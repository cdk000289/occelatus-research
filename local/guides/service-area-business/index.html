Guide · Trades & service businesses

Plumber, electrician or cleaner? How to set up Google Business Profile as a service-area business.

Plumbers, electricians, roofers, cleaners, landscapers and other businesses that go to the customer need a different profile setup from a shop. Getting it wrong is a commonly reported reason for suspensions and failed verification.

Updated November 10, 2026 · Plain-English guide for business owners

Short version: If customers don't visit you at your address, Google's guidelines say to hide it and list the areas you serve instead. Showing a home address, a mailbox or an address you can't staff, or stuffing keywords into the business name, are common reasons profiles get suspended or fail verification.

Do you need a service-area profile?

Google's guidelines, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google", separate businesses that serve customers at their location from those that travel to customers. Google's own example of the second group is a plumber working from home. If you work out of a house, garage or van and nobody comes to meet you, you are in that group. Its rules change from time to time, so read the current page.

The setup, step by step

  1. Hide the address. In your profile's business information, turn off showing your address to customers. You still enter it for Google's records, but it isn't shown publicly.
  2. Set your service area. Add the cities, ZIP codes or regions you actually cover. Google currently allows a limited number of areas and says they should not stretch too far from where you're based, so list only places you really serve.
  3. Use your real business name. Exactly as it appears on your sign, vehicle, invoices and website. No added keywords, phone numbers or cities.
  4. Pick the right primary category for what you mostly do, such as "Plumber" or "House cleaning service".
  5. Check phone and website match your other listings so Google can see one consistent business.

Mistakes that commonly cause trouble

  • Showing a home address on a profile where nobody visits. This is a direct mismatch with the service-area setup and also exposes your home on Maps.
  • A name like "Joe's Plumbing | 24/7 Emergency Plumber | Springfield". Only the real name belongs in that field.
  • A mailbox, virtual office or coworking desk as the address. These are hard or impossible to verify.
  • Listing a service area far from where you work to appear in more towns.
  • Creating a second profile after a problem. It usually makes a duplicate and more trouble.
Avoid: paying anyone who guarantees verification or reinstatement. Nobody outside Google can promise either outcome, and it isn't something we promise.

If you're already suspended or can't verify

Fix the mismatches above first, then follow our guides on failed verification and suspended profiles. Results vary and Google makes the final call.
