How to respond to wedding inquiries fast — without sounding like a template

The first vendor to reply usually wins. Here's what couples actually want in a first reply, three example replies you can adapt today, and a simple follow-up system that doesn't feel pushy.

By the Quill & Toast team · Updated October 2026 · ~8 min read

Why your reply speed matters more than your pricing

When a couple sends an inquiry, they're usually doing it to five or six vendors at once. They're not picking on price — they're testing who feels easy to work with. Vendor surveys consistently show that responding within the first hour doubles your booking rate compared to the vendor who replies the next morning.

But there's a tension: replying fast with a copy-pasted template makes you feel like a sales funnel, not a person. Couples can tell, and they often reply to the vendor who sounded human, not the one who replied fastest.

This guide covers what couples actually want in that first reply, three example replies adapted to different vendor types, and a follow-up system that feels like checking in — not chasing.

What couples want in a first reply

Based on vendor-client message reviews and wedding-industry surveys, the first reply that moves couples forward does five things:

  1. Acknowledges their date and event — "I'd love to be part of your October wedding!" feels personal; "Thank you for your inquiry" does not.
  2. Answers the availability question first — if your date is open, say so in the first line. If you're not sure yet (e.g. for a venue with held dates), say "I'm checking availability for October 12th and will confirm by end of day."
  3. Mentions their venue or location — shows you read the inquiry and know the area. "We've shot at The Emerson twice and love that courtyard" builds immediate trust.
  4. Gives a range, not a blank price — "Our full-day packages start at $2,800 and most couples land around $3,400 depending on coverage" is more helpful than "Please see our pricing guide attached" — and more honest than "Let's hop on a call to discuss."
  5. Asks one easy question — "How many guests are you expecting?" or "What's the vibe you're going for?" keeps the conversation moving without a phone-call gate.

The 5-minute rule: if you can draft a reply in five minutes that answers availability, references their venue, gives a price range, and asks one question — you'll book more than the vendor who spends 30 minutes crafting the perfect email but sends it tomorrow.

3 example replies you can adapt today

Each example below is the kind of reply that sounds like you wrote it in five minutes — not like a chatbot wrote it in five seconds. Use them as starting points, not scripts.

Wedding Photographer

Replying to "Do you have availability for October 12th?"

Hi Sarah — yes! October 12th is open on our calendar, and I'd love to be part of your day. I saw you mentioned The Emerson as your venue — we've shot there twice and the courtyard light in the late afternoon is gorgeous. You're in good hands there. Our full-day coverage starts at $2,800 and most couples add a second shooter or an album, landing around $3,400. I'm happy to send a quick PDF with the full breakdown if that's helpful. Quick question: how many guests are you planning for? That helps me suggest the right coverage level. Talk soon, — Jamie

Why it works: references the venue by name, gives a price range (not a wall of packages), one easy question to keep the thread alive.

Wedding/Event Planner

Replying to "We need help with day-of coordination"

Hi Mark — great to hear from you, and congratulations! Day-of coordination is one of our favorite services — you've done all the hard work planning, and our job is making sure the day runs smoothly so you can actually enjoy it. For a Saturday in October at Willow Ridge, our day-of package is $1,200 and includes a timeline walk-through two weeks before, vendor confirmation, and two coordinators on-site from setup through the last dance. We can also add rehearsal coordination for $200 if you'd like that. What does your guest count look like? And do you have a rough timeline put together yet, or is that still in progress? Happy to send a full breakdown, — Alex

Why it works: treats day-of as the win it is (not a downgrade), explains what's included so it's not a blank price, asks about timeline to move toward booking.

Wedding Venue

Replying to "Is October 12th available for a 120-guest wedding?"

Hi Jen — thank you for reaching out! October 12th is currently available, and 120 guests is right in our sweet spot — the Grand Hall handles up to 180 comfortably, so you'll have room to breathe. Our Saturday package is $9,500 and includes the ceremony space, reception hall, tables and chairs, and our preferred vendor list (catering, florals, and bar are separate — I can share estimates if helpful). We also include a 4-hour venue walkthrough as part of your booking. Would you like to come by for a tour? I have openings this Thursday at 2pm or Saturday at 11am if either works for you. Warmly, — Casey

Why it works: confirms the date immediately, puts the guest count in context (the room can hold it), gives the price upfront with what's included, offers concrete tour slots instead of an open-ended "let me know your availability."

A follow-up system that doesn't feel pushy

Most vendors either never follow up (losing couples who got busy) or follow up too aggressively (feeling like a salesperson). Here's a simple system that works:

Day 1: the first reply

Send the kind of reply above — availability, price range, one question. If they don't respond, that's fine. They're planning a wedding and they're busy.

Day 3: the soft nudge

One short, warm follow-up. Not "just checking in" — that's a nothing phrase. Instead, add something useful:

Hi Sarah — I know wedding planning keeps you busy! I wanted to share one thing that might be helpful: October is peak season at The Emerson, so if you're considering a few photographers, locking in your date soon gives you the most options. Happy to answer any questions whenever you're ready. — Jamie

Why it works: adds value (the seasonal availability tip), no pressure to book, no "are you still interested?" — just useful information and an open door.

Day 7: the final note

If they haven't replied by day 7, send one last warm note and move on. No guilt, no pressure:

Hi Sarah — I'll step back from your inbox! If October 12th is still on your radar, I'd love to be part of it. If you've gone in a different direction, I hope you have a beautiful wedding day. — Jamie

Why it works: graceful exit, leaves a good impression (couples talk to each other and recommend vendors who were kind), and doesn't burn the bridge.
