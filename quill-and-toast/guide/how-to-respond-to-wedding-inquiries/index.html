How to answer wedding inquiries fast, without sounding like a template

A couple who just sent an inquiry is waiting on you and probably on a few other vendors too. Here's what a good first reply covers, three examples you can borrow, and a follow-up routine that won't make you feel like a telemarketer.

By the Quill & Toast team · October 2026 · 6 min read

Speed matters. So does sounding like a person.

Most couples send the same inquiry to several vendors on the same night. Then they wait to see who writes back, and how. The vendor who answers on Saturday morning with real details is in a very different spot from the one who answers on Tuesday.

The trap is the canned reply. It's fast, but couples can spot "Thank you for your inquiry! Please find our pricing guide attached" from a mile away. It reads like a funnel. A reply that mentions their date and their venue reads like you.

So the goal is a reply that's quick and still obviously written for them.

What a good first reply covers

You don't need a long email. You need it to do five small things:

  1. Say something about their actual day. "An October wedding at Willow Ridge, lovely" beats "Thank you for reaching out."
  2. Deal with the date question early. If you've checked and you're free, say so up top. If you still need to check, say when you'll know. Silence on the date is what makes couples nervous.
  3. Mention their venue or area if you know it. One specific line proves you read the message.
  4. Give a real starting price. "Full-day coverage starts at $2,800, and most couples end up around $3,400" helps them more than a PDF they have to open, and it's more honest than "let's hop on a call."
  5. Ask one easy question, like guest count or what kind of day they're picturing. That keeps the thread going without forcing a phone call.

A useful test: could you write it in five minutes from your phone? If the reply covers their date, their venue, a starting price and one question, it's good enough to send now. A perfect email sent tomorrow is usually worse.

Three examples to borrow

These are written the way a busy owner would actually type them. The names, venues and prices are made up, so swap in your own.

Wedding photographer

"Do you have availability for October 12th?"

Hi Sarah, Good news, October 12th is open and I'd love to shoot it. The Emerson is a great pick. Ask for a few minutes in the courtyard around golden hour, the light back there is the best spot on the property. Full-day coverage starts at $2,800. Most couples add a second shooter or an album and land around $3,400. I can send the full breakdown if that's useful. How many guests are you expecting? That tells me which coverage makes sense. Talk soon, Jamie

Why it works: the date is answered in the first line, the venue line shows real attention, and there's a price and one question.

Wedding or event planner

"We need help with day-of coordination"

Hi Mark, congratulations! You've done the planning. Day-of is where we take it off your plate so you can actually be at your own wedding. For a Saturday in October at Willow Ridge, day-of coordination is $1,200. That covers a timeline walk-through two weeks out, confirming every vendor, and two of us on site from setup to the last song. Rehearsal coordination is another $200 if you want it. Roughly how many guests? And do you have a timeline yet, or is that still on the to-do list? Alex

Why it works: it treats day-of as a real service, spells out what the price includes, and the question nudges toward the next step.

Wedding venue

"Is October 12th available for 120 guests?"

Hi Jen, October 12th is open right now. 120 is a comfortable number for us, since the Grand Hall seats up to 180. Saturdays are $9,500. That includes the ceremony space, the reception hall, tables and chairs, and our preferred vendor list. Catering, flowers and bar are separate, and I can send rough numbers for those. Want to come see it? I have Thursday at 2pm or Saturday at 11am open for tours. Casey

Why it works: the date and capacity come first, the price says what's included, and the tour offer has two real times instead of "let me know when works."

Following up without being pushy

Two mistakes are common here. Some vendors never follow up, and lose couples who simply got busy. Others follow up every other day. A light, spaced routine works better.

Day 1: the first reply

Send something like the examples above. If you hear nothing back, don't read into it. They're planning a wedding.

Day 3: one useful nudge

Skip "just checking in." Give them something they can use:

Hi Sarah, one quick thing while you're comparing photographers: October dates at The Emerson tend to go early, so if you're leaning toward a few people, it's worth holding your date soon. Happy to answer anything whenever you get a minute. Jamie

Why it works: it's a real tip, it doesn't ask "are you still interested?", and it leaves the door open.

Day 7: the last note

If it's been a week, send one short, kind message and then let it go:

Hi Sarah, I'll get out of your inbox! If October 12th is still in the cards, I'd love to be part of it. And if you went another way, I hope it's a wonderful day. Jamie

Why it works: it's gracious, it ends on a nice note, and couples remember vendors who were pleasant even when they didn't book.
